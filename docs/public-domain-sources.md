# Public-domain sources for a kids' EN/FR/DE/IT dictionary (Sept 2026)

**Short answer:** buy an old printed copy for French, German and Italian and digitise it yourself. For English, much of it is already clean text on Project Gutenberg (PG). Before using any copy, check the title page so you know every author and reviser died before 1956.

**Labels:** **V** = verified. That means I saw it in the cited source or in a faithful copy of it; most live sites were blocked, so many links go to GitHub copies. **E** = my estimate or legal reasoning. **U** = unverified. Most death years below were checked, but some book details and all legal conclusions still need confirming. This is not legal advice.

## 1. Shortlist

Rule of thumb: in the UK and Switzerland (CH), a book is public domain (PD) if every author and editor died before 1 Jan 1956.

## 2. Buy and scan, or use existing scans?

- **English:** use the Gutenberg texts, and buy and scan the rest. The Internet Archive only allows access "for scholarship and research purposes only" V ([terms](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/saveweb/see-agreement/main/docs/%E6%9C%AA%E5%88%86%E7%B1%BB/Internet_Archive/index.en.md)). Google scans ask for non-commercial use only V ([guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/latin-ocr/00flgoog/master/00flgoog_0000.txt)).
- **French:** buy. Gallica charges for commercial reuse V ([terms](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SethGraebner/Paris-Project/master/xml/Guillemin_1852_bpt6k5442829x.xml)), and French law backs that fee V ([CRPA L324-2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/legalize-dev/legalize-fr/main/fr/LEGITEXT000031366350.md)). University of Toronto scans are the next best option.
- **German:** buy Eberhard and Schlessing. Otherwise use scans not made by Google.
- **Italian:** buy, or run your own OCR on the Internet Archive scan. Images from Italian state libraries can carry fees V ([Codice art. 108](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.legislativo:2004-01-22;42)).
- **Every copy:** photograph the title page and its reverse as proof of edition. Reject any copy that names a later reviser.

## 3. Legal must-knows

- **Digitising:**
  - Scanning or OCR does not create a new copyright in the text V ([US Copyright Office](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/adhocteam/copyright-compendium/main/copyright_compendium_pdfs/ch300-copyrightable-authorship.txt)).
  - The EU rule that copies of PD works get no new rights only covers visual art, not text V ([DSM Art. 14](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Shauryagulati/eu-law-agent/main/data/txt/CELEX_32019L0790_EN_TXT.txt)).
  - Switzerland protects photographs for 50 years V ([URG 2(3bis)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/droid-f/fedlex-assets/main/eli/cc/1993/1798_1798_1798/20250701/de/html/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1993-1798_1798_1798-20250701-de-html.html)). That covers only the images, and the scans you make yourself are yours.
- **Modern reprints:**
  - The UK protects the page layout of a printed edition for 25 years, but only against photographic copying. A photographic reprint of an old book gets no new protection V ([UK Copyright Act](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/legalize-dev/legalize-uk/main/uk/ukpga-1988-48.md); [NLA v M&S](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LegalWiseGenieKTH/thesis-legalresearch-hybrid/main/evaluation_dataset/raw/landmark/%5B2001%5D%20UKHL%2038.txt)).
  - New introductions and editorial work in a reprint can be protected V ([Sawkins](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LegalWiseGenieKTH/thesis-legalresearch-hybrid/main/evaluation_dataset/processed/landmark/%5B2005%5D%20EWCA%20Civ%20565_er.json); [German UrhG §70](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/legalize-dev/legalize-de/main/de/URHG.md)).
  - Retyping the text is fine. Don't ship page images from a recently reset reprint.
- **How long protection lasts:**
  - The UK only applies a foreign country's shorter term when none of the authors is British V ([UK Copyright Act s.12(6)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/legalize-dev/legalize-uk/main/uk/ukpga-1988-48.md)).
  - Switzerland reportedly never applies a shorter foreign term U ([overview](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutzfristenvergleich)). Confirm this with the Swiss IP office (IGE).
  - In the US, everything published before 1931 is PD V ([PG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrcook/gutenbergnews/master/content/posts/2026/2026-01-01-pg-monthly-newsletter-january-2026.md)). Foreign books published from 1931 on may have had US copyright restored for 95 years E ([Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ38b.pdf)).
- **Database right:**
  - The EU and UK protect databases for 15 years, but only when the maker is based there and invested substantially V ([EU Directive](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SFHAJJI/lex-articles/main/eu-eurlex/works/31996l0009/versions/2019-06-06--b6b8bff3a7fa9747e03d59cd5f9f417e25a72ae4a63719a2c5eb797418250284/en.md); [BHB case](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LegalWiseGenieKTH/thesis-legalresearch-hybrid/main/evaluation_dataset/processed/landmark/C-203%2002_er.json)). Modern digital editions of old dictionaries may qualify.
  - Switzerland has no database right, but its unfair-competition law bans copying someone else's finished product by technical means without real effort of your own V ([UWG 5(c)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/droid-f/fedlex-assets/main/eli/cc/1988/223_223_223/20250101/de/html/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1988-223_223_223-20250101-de-html.html)).
  - A website's terms only bind the people who agreed to them V ([ProCD](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Hollaar/digital-law-online/master/cases/39PQ2D1161.htm)).
- **Rewriting with AI:**
  - Feeding in PD text is safe. The risk is output that repeats wording from modern dictionaries; in the EU even 11 words can count as copying V ([Infopaq](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LegalWiseGenieKTH/thesis-legalresearch-hybrid/main/evaluation_dataset/processed/landmark/C-5%2008_er.json)).
  - The UK rule that gives copyright to computer-generated text may be repealed (U, [secondary source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jamie-lord/Lord.technology/master/_posts/2026-05-03-the-uk-already-answered-who-owns-ai-generated-code.md)).
  - The US requires a human author (U, [secondary source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rhundhausen/whoownsthecode/main/content/developments/supreme-court-denies-cert-thaler.md)).
  - Under Anthropic's commercial terms the customer owns the outputs and gets an IP indemnity V ([terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)).
  - Have a person edit every entry, and record which book each one came from.

## 4. Pitfalls

- **Old German type (Fraktur):**
  - Tesseract's `deu_latf` and `script/Fraktur` models are free for commercial use (Apache-2.0) V ([repo](https://github.com/tesseract-ocr/tessdata_best/commits/main/deu_latf.traineddata)).
  - Calamari's historical models were trained on CC BY/BY-SA data, so their licensing is unclear V ([wiki](https://github.com/tesseract-ocr/tesstrain/wiki/GT4HistOCR)).
- **Spelling:**
  - German: modernise pre-1901 forms (Thür, Thal) and pre-1996 forms (daß). Swiss German writes ss, never ß (U, [sok.ch](https://sok.ch/2007/11/eszett-in-der-schweiz/)).
  - French: schools in French-speaking Switzerland have taught the 1990 reformed spelling as the standard since 2023 V ([IRDP](https://www.irdp.ch/institut/langue-francaise-vivante-orthographe-aussi-3768.html)). Add the Swiss numbers septante, huitante and nonante V ([source](https://francaisdenosregions.com/2024/11/16/septante-et-nonante/)).
- **Content:**
  - Racist, colonial and sexist meanings and examples need a human filter.
  - Nothing after about 1930 is covered, so modern words must be added by hand. For English, WordNet's licence allows commercial use V ([licence](https://wordnet.princeton.edu/license-and-commercial-use)).
  - Library catalogue dates can be wrong, for example in the Digital Library of India V ([example](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.14785)). Trust the title page.
